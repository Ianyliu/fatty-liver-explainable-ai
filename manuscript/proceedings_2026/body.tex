\section{Introduction}
An ultrasound examination provides multiple views of a patient's tissues. Combining those views can improve the information available to a diagnostic model, but a patient-level prediction does not reveal which images support or oppose it. Grouped-image neural networks offer a natural representation: images become graph nodes, relations between their representations become edges, and a graph-level output summarizes the examination. \citet{yen2024fattyliver} developed this approach for fatty-liver classification. Our objective is to explain a fixed instance of that model at the image level.

Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) approximates a prediction model using interpretable perturbations \citep{ribeiro2016lime}. For a single image, perturbations commonly remove superpixels. A multi-image examination instead offers a clinically recognizable unit of explanation: an individual ultrasound view. We adapt the perturbation principle to image subsets and their reconstructed graphs. A binary inclusion matrix connects each subset's model response to the images it contains. This produces an explanation of the grouped prediction without changing or retraining the GNN.

The proposed framework combines two complementary views of image influence. Regularized surrogate models describe conditional linear associations between image inclusion and the model's probability response; Pearson correlations describe marginal associations. These summaries need not agree when images or their inclusion patterns are correlated. Additive attribution methods \citep{lundberg2017shap} and graph-specific subgraph selection \citep{ying2019gnnexplainer} provide related perspectives, but neither is implemented as a comparator here.

A further methodological component is two-stage Adaptive Class-Balanced Sampling. Random subsets may repeatedly receive the same predicted class, limiting the range of behavior available to an explanation. The adaptive scheme uses singleton-predicted image pools to favor the class underrepresented among sampled subset predictions. The intended balance is a sampling objective rather than a guarantee: a collection of individually positive-predicted images need not yield a positive subset prediction, and finite pool capacities constrain the requested composition.

We present the explanation workflow, an aligned patient-level visualization of marginal and conditional influence, and an exploratory evaluation of fidelity, sampling feasibility, deletion behavior and repeatability. The ten-patient convenience pilot was inspected before extending the unchanged main evaluation to all 135 eligible positive-label patients. The expansion includes the pilot and is not an independent replication. Evaluation is intended to identify when the explanation framework is informative and where its assumptions are limited, rather than to establish clinical validity.

\section{Image-subset explanation framework}
\subsection{Grouped-ultrasound prediction}
The supplied model uses an ImageNet-pretrained DenseNet121 to encode each image and a \texttt{SETNET\_GAT} network to aggregate the image set. Grayscale images are converted to three channels, resized to $224\times224$, and normalized using the ImageNet convention. Correlation between image feature vectors defines graph edges at the implemented threshold $>0.95$. The GAT layer supplies self-loops, followed by an MLP, a linear node transformation, mean pooling and the final two-class output. The trained checkpoint and preprocessing remain fixed throughout the explanation experiments.

For patient $i$ with $n_i$ images, let $u\in\{0,1\}^{n_i}$ record which images are included and let $G_i(u)$ be the corresponding reconstructed graph. The explanation response is the class-1 softmax probability,
\begin{equation}q_i(u)=\Pr_{\mathrm{GNN}}(\text{class }1\mid G_i(u)).\end{equation}
The full-set prediction uses $u=\mathbf{1}$. Applying the same model to each image separately partitions the examination into singleton-predicted class-0 and class-1 pools. These are model predictions, not observed image-level disease labels. Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} reproduces the original conceptual workflow; its caption identifies historical labels that differ from the current evaluation.

\subsection{Two-stage adaptive sampling}
Both random and adaptive arms receive 1,000 training draws per patient--seed. Random sampling draws subset size uniformly from the integers $3,\ldots,n_i$, then selects distinct images without replacement. A mask can recur on different draws; repeated masks remain separate training rows and consume fresh model calls.

Adaptive sampling requests 500 subset predictions in each model class. Stage I samples randomly while both quotas remain unmet. Once one quota is reached, Stage II favors the singleton-predicted pool associated with the deficient class. The intended pool mixture is 85/15. Integer counts follow the implemented rounding rule, and the positive-pool count is clamped to the interval allowed by the available images. Pool deficits are reallocated to preserve subset size; a single available pool triggers random fallback. Target composition, realized composition and achieved prediction balance are therefore distinct quantities.

The comparison matches training-row budgets. Adaptive sampling incurs additional singleton inference and constructor-validation calls, so equal row counts do not imply equal computational cost. Sampling and prediction details were fixed before the pilot and retained for the expansion.

\subsection{Conditional and marginal image influence}
Fixed-alpha Ridge regression \citep{hoerl1970ridge} estimates
\begin{equation}(\widehat b,\widehat\beta)=\arg\min_{b,\beta}\left[\sum_{r=1}^{1000}\{q_i(u_r)-b-u_r^\mathsf{T}\beta\}^2+\|\beta\|_2^2\right].\end{equation}
The intercept is fitted without penalty, inclusion columns are unscaled, and alpha is 1. A positive coefficient associates image inclusion with a higher class-1 probability conditional on the other indicators in the surrogate. This is a linear approximation under the sampled distribution; it is not an isolated biological effect. No locality kernel is used, and surrogate scores are neither constrained nor clipped to $[0,1]$.

The ten-patient analysis also uses Elastic Net probability regression \citep{zou2005elasticnet}. Training-only shuffled five-fold CV minimizes mean squared error over 25 log-spaced penalties from $10^{-4}$ to $1$ and mixing ratios $0.1,0.5,0.9,1$. The fold seed matches the training seed. Predictors remain unscaled, an intercept is fitted, and convergence warnings fail the fit. Evaluation responses are excluded from model selection.

Pearson correlation between each image's inclusion indicator and $q_i(u)$ provides a marginal association. It is unavailable for zero-variance variables. Marginal and conditional summaries can differ because a marginal association includes co-inclusion patterns, whereas regularized regression conditions on the remaining indicators. Their units also differ: Pearson correlation is dimensionless, while coefficients describe changes on the probability-response scale.

\section{Data and evaluation design}
\subsection{Data provenance and populations}
The original publication identifies Taiwan Biobank as the dataset provider and MacKay Memorial Hospital Health Examination Center in Taipei as the acquisition site \citep{yen2024fattyliver}. We use the supplied test09 split and patient metadata. Eligibility requires a positive observed liver-fat label, at least 20 metadata-listed images, and complete image availability. The eligible population comprises 135 patients and 3,072 images, with 20--35 images per patient. These restrictions do not establish clinical representativeness or complete coverage of the broader test set.

The preliminary ten-patient convenience cohort was ordered by image count and then identifier; each patient has 20 images. Inspection of this pilot preceded expansion of the same main protocol to the remaining 125 eligible patients. Ridge fidelity and deletion are evaluated in the main study; Elastic Net, Pearson ranking diagnostics, repeatability and leave-one-image-out comparisons remain ten-patient analyses. \subsection{Probability-surrogate fidelity}
An independent RNG stream generates 200 random evaluation draws per patient--seed, shared by both arms. Primary evaluation retains draws whose masks occurred in neither training arm, preserving multiplicity. If $\mathcal E_{is}$ is this shared-novel multiset, the error is
\begin{equation}\mathrm{MAE}_{isa}=|\mathcal E_{is}|^{-1}\sum_{u\in\mathcal E_{is}}|q_i(u)-\widehat q_{isa}(u)|,\end{equation}
where $s$ denotes seed and $a$ denotes sampling arm. Lower MAE indicates better approximation on this evaluation distribution. Each constant baseline equals its arm's training-mean GNN probability and is evaluated on the same rows. All-draw MAE, RMSE, class support and out-of-range scores provide additional diagnostics.

Novel-mask filtering conditions the evaluation on masks unseen in both arms and can remove large subsets preferentially. Empty evaluation sets produce unavailable metrics, never zero error. One-class sets limit the usefulness of class agreement even when probability errors remain defined. Counts and availability accompany the results.

We average seeds 0, 1 and 2 within each patient, then weight patients equally. A primary patient mean requires all three paired seed results. Missing patients make the complete-cohort primary mean unavailable; available-patient results are separately identified. SD is the descriptive sample SD of patient means. No inferential confidence intervals or significance tests are used.

\subsection{Deletion-based model behavior}
Images are ranked by signed class-1 Ridge coefficients in descending and ascending order, with a seeded random-order control shared across sampling arms. Ties use a fixed image-ID rule. Nominal deletion fractions are $0,0.1,\ldots,0.5$; deletion counts are floored, at least three nodes are retained, and repeated counts are evaluated once. Every retained subset receives graph reconstruction and fresh inference.

Raw deletion AUC integrates class-1 probability over the actual deleted fractions using the trapezoidal rule. Descending-minus-control contrasts are paired within patient--seed and then averaged within patient. A negative contrast indicates a lower class-1 trajectory after removal of the largest positive coefficients. Curves need not be monotone, and unequal patient-specific grids are not pooled as identical. Normalized AUC is supplementary. These interventions assess model behavior rather than clinical or causal importance.

\subsection{Additional explanation diagnostics}
Ten-patient repeatability is measured by Spearman rank correlation, top-five Jaccard similarity and sign agreement across the three seed pairs. Constant or undefined rankings remain unavailable. Leave-one-image-out (LOO) measures full-set minus omitted-image class-1 probability under graph reconstruction. Its agreement with surrogate and marginal rankings is descriptive: LOO concerns the neighborhood of the full set, whereas the surrogate spans many subset sizes.

The analysis independently checks training masks and responses, surrogate fits, evaluation membership and deletion integration. The pilot review refitted 60 Ridge and 60 Elastic Net models and verified CV selections. Design conditioning and realized pool composition are calculated from preserved training outputs. Further validation and historical-method differences are documented in the supplement.

\section{Discussion}
This framework extends image perturbation from parts of a single image to views within a patient-level graph. The binary design supplies an interpretable link between the grouped GNN and regularized image-inclusion summaries. Conditional coefficients and marginal correlations offer complementary descriptions that can be displayed alongside the original ultrasound views. Their comparison is useful precisely because the summaries answer different questions about model behavior.

Adaptive Class-Balanced Sampling attempts to broaden the predicted-class support of the perturbations through two stages. Its motivation does not establish its empirical advantage. The pilot showed poorer mean fidelity under adaptive sampling and failed to attain the requested prediction balance. Singleton classes do not determine subset classes, graph interactions can change the response, and capacity reallocation alters the intended pool mixture. The observed feasibility and fidelity results therefore remain essential qualifications to the proposed sampling strategy. \PrimaryInterpretationText{}

The perturbation distribution is part of the explanation's meaning. A coefficient describes the model under the sampled co-inclusion patterns and subset sizes, rather than an image's unique biological contribution. Regularization addresses conditioning and coefficient variability but does not resolve that interpretation. Near-constant responses can also yield undefined correlations or all-zero Elastic Net vectors; deterministic tie breaking does not make these informative explanations.

Deletion interventions add a complementary check by asking whether removing positively ranked views changes the model response more than control removals. Lower probability trajectories support a model-behavior interpretation of the ranking under that intervention. They do not establish diagnostic relevance, and favorable deletion behavior does not guarantee small surrogate error. LOO addresses a still narrower intervention near the full image set.

Limitations include restriction to complete positive-label patients, one split/checkpoint and a convenience pilot followed by expansion after results were seen. Repeated seeds measure within-patient repeatability, not independent patient replication. The methods have unequal inference overhead, and one random deletion order per seed does not characterize the full random-control distribution. The supplied implementation uses a feature-correlation threshold greater than 0.95, whereas the source publication describes 0.995. These results concern the supplied fixed model rather than exact reproduction of every published setting or its conformal-risk procedure. No physician evaluation or external clinical validation was performed.

Further work could compare matched total-query budgets, vary pool ratios and subset sizes, repeat random deletion controls, and expand the supplementary explanation methods. Such experiments are distinct from the completed analyses and are not required to interpret the present findings.

\section{Conclusion}
LIME-inspired image-subset perturbations provide a reproducible way to examine a grouped-ultrasound GNN through conditional and marginal image-level explanations. Aligned visualizations make those complementary associations accessible, while paired fidelity, feasibility, deletion and repeatability analyses delimit their interpretation. Adaptive sampling is a proposed component whose performance must be evaluated rather than assumed; the present evaluation retains unfavorable results and supports no claim of clinical or causal image importance.
