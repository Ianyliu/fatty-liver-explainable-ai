\section{Sampling distribution and adaptive allocation}\label{app:sampling}
For one patient, suppress $i$ and let $n$ denote the number of images. A random mask has probability
\begin{equation}
\Pr_R(\boldsymbol U=\boldsymbol u)=
\frac{1}{n-2}\binom{n}{k}^{-1},\qquad
k=\sum_{j=1}^n u_j\in\{3,\ldots,n\}.
\label{eq:maskprob}
\end{equation}
This follows by multiplying the uniform probability of size $k$ by the probability of selecting that particular $k$-image subset. Consequently, masks are equiprobable within a size but not across sizes. Nearly complete masks have few alternatives and can recur frequently.

Let $z_i(\boldsymbol u)$ be the model's predicted class and define singleton pools
\begin{equation}
P_i^+=\{j:z_i(\boldsymbol e_j)=1\},\quad
P_i^-=\{j:z_i(\boldsymbol e_j)=0\},\quad
p_i=|P_i^+|,\quad m_i=|P_i^-|.
\end{equation}
Here $\boldsymbol e_j$ retains image $j$ alone. Before draw $r$, accumulated subset-prediction counts are
\begin{equation}
N_c(r-1)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{r-1}\mathbf 1\{z_i(\boldsymbol u_\ell)=c\},
\qquad c\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
With $B=1{,}000$ and $T_0=T_1=500$, the draw is random when both $N_c<T_c$, or when either singleton pool is empty. Otherwise Stage II sets
\begin{equation}
\rho_r=\begin{cases}0.85,&N_1(r-1)<T_1,\\0.15,&N_1(r-1)\ge T_1,\end{cases}
\qquad K_r\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{3,\ldots,n_i\},
\end{equation}
and samples $c_i(K_r,\rho_r)$ images from $P_i^+$ and $K_r-c_i(K_r,\rho_r)$ from $P_i^-$. Counts are updated from the resulting grouped prediction. Sampling stops at the fixed row budget; it does not continue until balance is attained.

During Stage II, conditional on $K_r$, the count history and two nonempty pools, each feasible subset of the specified pool composition has probability
\begin{equation}
\Pr_A(\mathcal X_i(\boldsymbol U_r)=S\mid K_r,\mathcal H_{r-1})=
\left\{\binom{p_i}{c_i}\binom{m_i}{K_r-c_i}\right\}^{-1},
\end{equation}
with probability zero for other compositions. The history $\mathcal H_{r-1}$ determines which class is deficient. Reallocation occurs precisely when $c_i\ne\lfloor\rho_rK_r\rfloor$. Requested pool composition and prediction-class balance are therefore different features of the procedure.

\section{Regularized surrogate objectives}\label{app:regression}
For a design $U\in\{0,1\}^{B\times n}$ and responses $\boldsymbol q$, define centered quantities
\begin{equation}
M=I_B-B^{-1}\boldsymbol1\boldsymbol1^{\mathsf T},\qquad
\widetilde U=MU,\qquad \widetilde{\boldsymbol q}=M\boldsymbol q.
\end{equation}
The unpenalized intercept permits the Ridge solution
\begin{align}
\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_R&=
(\widetilde U^{\mathsf T}\widetilde U+\lambda I_n)^{-1}
\widetilde U^{\mathsf T}\widetilde{\boldsymbol q},\quad\lambda=1,\\
\widehat b_R&=\bar q-\bar{\boldsymbol u}^{\mathsf T}\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}_R.
\end{align}
If $d_j$ are the singular values of $\widetilde U$, the coefficient along each right-singular direction is multiplied by $d_j/(d_j^2+\lambda)$ instead of the least-squares factor $1/d_j$. This is the source of shrinkage. The slope effective degrees of freedom are
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{df}_{\mathrm{slope}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{d_j^2}{d_j^2+\lambda}.
\end{equation}

The Elastic Net fit uses the objective
\begin{equation}
\min_{b,\boldsymbol\beta}
\left\{\frac{1}{2B}\|\boldsymbol q-b\boldsymbol1-U\boldsymbol\beta\|_2^2
+\alpha\eta\|\boldsymbol\beta\|_1
+\frac{\alpha(1-\eta)}{2}\|\boldsymbol\beta\|_2^2\right\}.
\label{eq:enet}
\end{equation}
The mixing parameter $\eta=1$ gives the Lasso penalty \citep{tibshirani1996lasso} and smaller values add Ridge shrinkage \citep{zou2005elasticnet}. Its $1/(2B)$ loss scaling differs from equation~\eqref{eq:ridge}, so numerically identical penalty parameters need not represent identical shrinkage. We selected $\alpha$ from 25 logarithmically spaced values in $[10^{-4},1]$ and $\eta\in\{0.1,0.5,0.9,1\}$ by shuffled five-fold training-only cross-validation minimizing mean squared error. An intercept was fitted, predictors were not standardized, and no evaluation responses entered selection. All training rows had equal weight and no locality kernel or probability clipping was applied.

\section{Marginal association and subset size}\label{app:marginal}
For image $j$, the sample Pearson coefficient is
\begin{equation}
\widehat r_j=
\frac{\sum_{r=1}^B(u_{rj}-\bar u_j)(q_r-\bar q)}
{\{\sum_{r=1}^B(u_{rj}-\bar u_j)^2
\sum_{r=1}^B(q_r-\bar q)^2\}^{1/2}}.
\end{equation}
It is undefined when either variable has zero variance. For a binary indicator $U_j$ with inclusion probability $\pi_j$ and a nonconstant response $Q$,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cor}(U_j,Q)=
\frac{\sqrt{\pi_j(1-\pi_j)}}{\operatorname{SD}(Q)}
\{\operatorname E(Q\mid U_j=1)-\operatorname E(Q\mid U_j=0)\}.
\end{equation}
Thus correlation compares the average responses of two collections of subsets whose remaining images may differ. It is not the regression coefficient conditional on the other inclusion indicators.

Under the random design, exchangeability gives $\operatorname E(U_j\mid K)=K/n$. Therefore
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Cov}(U_j,K)
&=\operatorname E\{K\operatorname E(U_j\mid K)\}
-\operatorname E(U_j)\operatorname E(K)\\
&=\operatorname{Var}(K)/n.
\end{align}
For $K\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{3,\ldots,n\}$,
\begin{equation}
\operatorname E(K)=\frac{n+3}{2},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(K)=\frac{(n-2)^2-1}{12}.
\end{equation}
For $n>3$, this covariance is positive. Its contribution to marginal response association depends on how $q_i(\boldsymbol u)$ varies with size. The identity applies to random sampling while adaptive pool selection changes the inclusion distribution.

\section{Evaluation and patient-level aggregation}\label{app:evaluation}
Let $\mathcal T_{isR}$ and $\mathcal T_{isA}$ be the sets of training masks, and let $(\boldsymbol v_\ell)_{\ell=1}^{200}$ be the independent random evaluation draws. The retained evaluation multiset is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_{is}=\{\boldsymbol v_\ell:\boldsymbol v_\ell\notin
\mathcal T_{isR}\cup\mathcal T_{isA}\},
\end{equation}
where membership is checked by mask and repeated evaluation draws retain their multiplicity. The arm-specific constant is $\bar q_{isa}=B^{-1}\sum_rq_i(\boldsymbol u_{isar})$. Its MAE uses the same $\mathcal E_{is}$ as the fitted surrogate. Since few distinct masks exist at sizes close to $n_i$, novelty filtering can alter the size distribution of the evaluation sets.

For any metric $h_{isa}$ available at all three seeds,
\begin{equation}
\bar h_{ia}=\frac13\sum_{s=0}^2h_{isa},\quad
\bar h_a=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\bar h_{ia},\quad
\overline\Delta=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N(\bar h_{iA}-\bar h_{iR}).
\end{equation}
Dispersion is the sample standard deviation across patient means, rather than across subsets or seeds. For partially defined rank metrics, available-patient summaries use the stated number of patients with complete seed comparisons. Undefined values are not assigned zero.

For deletion order $o$, let $d_t$ be distinct integer deletion counts obtained by flooring nominal fractions of $n_i$ and retaining at least three images. Put $f_t=d_t/n_i$ and let $q_t^{(o)}$ be the response after removal of the first $d_t$ images. Raw AUC is
\begin{equation}
A_{isa}^{(o)}=
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{q_{t-1}^{(o)}+q_t^{(o)}}2(f_t-f_{t-1}).
\end{equation}
The grid is patient-specific and spans at most $0.5$, so $0\le A^{(o)}\le0.5$. Descending-minus-random and descending-minus-ascending contrasts are formed before averaging seeds. One seeded random order per patient and seed is shared by the training schemes. Tied coefficients are ordered by a fixed image-label rule.

Single-image omission effects are
\begin{equation}
\delta_{ij}=q_i(\boldsymbol1)-q_i(\boldsymbol1-\boldsymbol e_j),\qquad
D_i=\frac1{n_i}\sum_{j=1}^{n_i}|\delta_{ij}|.
\end{equation}
Ranking--LOO agreement is the Spearman correlation between the fitted image estimates and $\boldsymbol\delta_i$, averaged over training seeds. A constant effect or ranking vector makes this correlation undefined. The full-set and omission predictions use one evaluated baseline per patient.

Repeatability compares the three seed pairs. Spearman correlation uses average ranks for ties. If $T_s$ contains the five highest-ranked images at seed $s$, top-five overlap is $|T_s\cap T_{s'}|/|T_s\cup T_{s'}|$. Sign agreement is the fraction of images whose signs match: estimates with absolute value at most $10^{-10}$ are treated as zero. Pair-specific scores are averaged within patient, requiring defined nonconstant vectors for all seed pairs.

\section{Software and computational details}\label{app:software}
Analyses were implemented in Python, with scikit-learn for regularized regression \citep{pedregosa2011sklearn}, NumPy/SciPy for numerical summaries, PyTorch for prediction, and Matplotlib for figure generation. The image encoder used ImageNet-pretrained DenseNet121 weights. Grayscale images were converted to three channels, resized to $224\times224$ and normalized using ImageNet means and standard deviations. The 1,024-dimensional representations entered one graph-attention layer with one head and 512 output features. Graph edges connected image representations with Pearson feature correlation greater than $0.95$. Negative or undefined correlations did not create edges, and self-loops were supplied by the attention layer. Two 512-dimensional linear transformations with batch normalization and ReLU, followed by a 1,024-dimensional linear transformation and ReLU, preceded mean pooling and the two-class output. Dropout was disabled during inference.

Each training scheme used 405,000 subset predictions across 135 patients and three seeds. Adaptive sampling additionally required 9,216 singleton predictions and 405 full-set validation predictions. Evaluation and shared random-deletion predictions were counted once across schemes. The sampling, Ridge-fidelity and deletion evaluation used 911,151 model calls; selected LOO baselines used 3,207 calls. Runtime records preserve exact software versions, input hashes, numerical validation and query accounting alongside the reproducibility scripts.
