# Explainable Disease Classification via Multi-Ultrasound Images using Graph Neural Networks

**Abstract:**

Deep learning models, particularly Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), have shown great promise in medical image analysis, such as classifying diseases from multiple ultrasound images per patient. However, the lack of interpretability of these black-box models hinders their clinical adoption. This work proposes a novel Explainable AI (XAI) framework that extends Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) to GNN-based multi-instance learning settings. Unlike traditional LIME which operates on superpixels of a single image, our method samples subgraphs of images to provide image-level importance scores. We introduce a "two-stage adaptive class-balanced sampling" strategy based on image clustering to generate meaningful variations of the input graph. To derive explanations, we employ Ridge and Elastic Net regression as local interpretable models, allowing us to quantify the conditional influence of each image on the model's prediction. Furthermore, we compute marginal relationships using Pearson correlation to complement the conditional view. A key feature of our framework is the incorporation of uncertainty quantification via bootstrapping, providing confidence intervals and standard errors for the importance scores. We demonstrate the efficacy of our approach on the classification of fatty liver disease using multiple ultrasound images, offering clinicians intuitive visualizations of both marginal and conditional image importance with statistical significance indicators.
